Separate the outcome from the reasons

Community accounts. The 2023 debrief brings together food experiences, heat, equipment, passing conflicts, rest and getting home. It shows why a distance alone cannot explain how an event went.[2]

Editorial conclusion. A useful personal debrief records the conditions and the decisions that changed the day, including what worked well.[2]

Keep contradictory experiences visible

Community accounts. Some participants describe the provided dinner as too heavy; another says the solid meal carried them through the night. Others differ about when to stop, what shoes to wear and how much nutrition to carry.[2] [3]

Editorial conclusion. A useful knowledgebank preserves these differences with attribution instead of averaging them into advice nobody actually gave.[2] [3]

Include safety and the journey home

Community accounts. Repeated accounts in the debrief describe near-sleep or difficulty driving after the event. Riders also raise unclear pit entry, fast passing and difficulty identifying first-aid provision. These belong in the planning questions for the next edition. The source comments are historical reports, not current statements about the organiser’s facilities or rules.[2]

Write the notes while you remember

Community accounts. One contributor explicitly suggests keeping a journal to read before packing for the next event. A useful structure is: keep, change, test and confirm. Keep what worked; change one clearly identified problem; test uncertain gear or food before relying on it; confirm event-specific arrangements with the organiser.[2]

The same distance can be a different outcome

Community accounts. One inline rider described stopping at 150 miles in 2023, reaching 250 in 2024 but being too uncomfortable to enjoy the awards, then reaching 250 again in 2025 and walking onto the stage. They did not explain enough changes to establish why recovery differed. The account still adds a useful debrief question: how did you finish, and what could you comfortably do afterward, alongside the distance total?[1]

Make it practical

Editorial prompts based on the discussion above.

  • What worked, and in which conditions?
  • What stopped or slowed you, and what evidence explains it?
  • What will you test before changing the next plan?
  • Which event details need fresh confirmation?
