Board and equipment

Community accounts. The packing thread names the board and helmet as the starting point, then adds spares and extra gear in case of failure. The support discussion also mentions a spare board where available.[1] [6]

Editorial conclusion. This is a prompt to bring compatible tools and parts, not a requirement to buy a second complete setup.[1] [6]

Conditions and comfort

Community accounts. Contributors list spare shoes, dry and warm clothes, a headlight, sunglasses, sunscreen and a power bank. A tent is discussed both as a sleeping place and a way to keep equipment dry. Night riding at the Dutch event adds a specific suggestion: clear glasses for insects, alongside daytime eyewear.[3] [6]

Food access and the small things

Community accounts. Riders describe jersey pockets, hydration bags, prepared bottles and food variety. One packing reply adds a toothbrush because hours of sweet foods and drinks can become unpleasant.[4] [6]

Editorial conclusion. These details illustrate why practising access and comfort matters as much as putting enough items in a bag.[4] [6]

Sleep and after the finish

Community accounts. The support thread includes eye masks, earplugs, chairs and sleeping pads. The 2023 debrief repeatedly raises unsafe post-event driving. Make the arrangements for leaving and resting before the event, rather than leaving that decision until you are exhausted.[1] [5]

Equipment for people who are waiting

Reported experience. For teenagers staying trackside, one parent recommended canopy shelter, chairs, blankets, books, phone batteries and extra snacks. They had given bulky purchased equipment to other attendees afterward. Another member offered a loan through a friend on a pay-it-forward basis. These show possible ways to handle overseas luggage limits; they do not establish an equipment rental or lending service.[2]

Reported experience. A supporter also pointed out that sitting through sun and a cool night creates different needs from skating. Their list included their own food, iced cooler, drinks, seating and layers, plus clean clothes, wipes and a towel for after the event. Arrange the crew’s supplies as deliberately as the rider’s.[1]

Make it practical

Editorial prompts based on the discussion above.

  • Board, helmet, compatible tools and essential spares.
  • Tested shoes, spare dry layers and weather protection.
  • Lights, charging cables, power bank and any spare batteries.
  • Food, drinks, containers and a resupply system.
  • Crew seating, shelter and rest supplies where permitted.
  • A confirmed place to recover and a plan for transport.
