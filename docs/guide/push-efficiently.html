What riders say about regular, mongo, switch and pumping over a long day.

There is no single switching rhythm

Community accounts. The push-technique discussion contains three-to-five-push sets with occasional mongo when tired, counting in fours to reduce mental effort, and a six-part sequence alternating regular, mongo, switch and switch mongo in sets of five. The detailed five-push rider also considered changing the pattern with track sections. These are rehearsed approaches, not evidence that one count is best.[6]

Foot placement is part of the effort

Community accounts. One participant describes reducing the repeated turn of the front foot from forward-facing to sideways and back. The original questioner later reports trying that placement and noticing less extra motion. This is an exchange about what worked for them, not a universal technique standard.[1]

Pumping can be a change of movement, not just a speed target

Community accounts. A rider asking about bracket boards describes an 80% push / 20% pump pattern and using pumping to break up the pushing. Responses focus on comfort with the existing board. Another first-timer found their board too high and unstable for the event and stopped pumping early.[3] [4]

Editorial conclusion. Together these stories suggest comparing movement and comfort on your own setup before making equipment assumptions.[3] [4]

Keep a margin for control

Community accounts. The 2023 debrief includes a wheelbite incident during enthusiastic switch pumping. It also contains concern about fast passing and pit-entry conflicts between disciplines. These are reasons to treat clearance, predictable movement and the event briefing as part of technique preparation. Follow the current event briefing for lane and passing rules.[5]

A useful cue still needs an observable result

Opinion. A participant used lifting their knees as a reminder to make a stronger push, saying they otherwise became lazy about it. They did not report force, speed or movement measurements. It is a personal cue to examine, not a proven rule that higher knees always produce more power.[2]

Editorial conclusion. When trying a cue, distinguish what you changed from what you noticed. Comfort, control and repeatability are more useful observations than assuming an energetic-looking movement must be efficient.

Make it practical

Editorial prompts based on the discussion above.

  • Compare several switching patterns during practice.
  • Notice unnecessary foot shuffling and upper-body motion.
  • Check your setup for clearance through the range of movement you use.
  • Confirm passing and pit-entry procedures at the current event briefing.
