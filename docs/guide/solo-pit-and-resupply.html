A solo pit and a workable resupply routine

Containers, reach, heat and discipline can matter as much as the food inside.

Solo does not mean refusing all help

Reported experience. Several riders planned solo participation with a box of food and drinks; one says their best distance came without support because they kept skating. That same reply still offered RV/cooler help, showing solo need not mean refusing all assistance. No controlled comparison of crew effectiveness.[5]

Reported experience. A rider left food in a box without a lid and birds ate it; a covered storage system would have addressed this specific failure. Closing food containers is editorial application of the incident.[5]

Editorial conclusion. A covered box directly addresses the reported bird problem. Arrange help you can actually count on rather than assuming a neighbour will always be available.

A bottle grab is different with a paddle

Reported experience. A 2023 reply described large iced water coolers trackside and expected quick bladder refills. Another used disposable bottles, half Tailwind and half plain water, lined along the wall to grab without stepping off. The paddling questioner felt carrying a paddle made that handoff too difficult. Historical availability; practical coordination differs by discipline.[3]

Reported experience. A solo pit can be arranged for a bottle grab every lap; one rider planned a canopy to prevent supplies heating. A paddler instead carried hydration because a paddle hindered bottle grabs and stopping sacrificed tailwind momentum; they reported stops every five laps and completing a 50-mile goal.[2]

Editorial conclusion. Compare a bottle handoff, a carried reservoir and a deliberate stop in the context of your discipline. Use the crew guide for communication and preparation roles.

Do not build the meal plan around assumed electricity

Reported experience. Before the 2022 Dutch Ultra, a respondent recalled kettles being disallowed because a high load might trip the circuit shared with timing; others recalled gas cookers being allowed and offered to share one. The asker had already planned food around not needing hot water. These are recollections, not current electrical or flame permissions.[4]

Editorial conclusion. The old kettle and shared-circuit account is a question for the current organiser, not permission to use a stove. Have a meal and charging plan that matches confirmed facilities.

If coffee matters to you, plan how it arrives

Reported experience. Riders described coffee as scarce enough at their past event to bring their own. One suggested a coffee machine; another bought a cold brewer to prepare a batch in advance. The latter believed it was less acidic, but the discussion did not test that claim. The useful logistical difference is between needing an appliance at the track and arriving with a drink already prepared.[1]

Editorial conclusion. Check the current supply and appliance permissions. A reported wall outlet does not settle whether your coffee equipment is permitted or practical.
