Shoes, grip and comfort over many hours

Minimalist shoes, skate shoes and running shoes all have advocates—and counterexamples.

Read both sides of the barefoot-shoe discussion

Community accounts. A primarily paddling push/paddle rider with wide feet liked Xero shoes used in training and at the previous Ultra. Another reported a 156-mile personal best in Merrell Vapor Glove 3 shoes but considered the soles too thin to use again. A third credited different Merrell barefoot models with resolving their earlier discomfort, after an adaptation period. A fourth changed from minimalist shoes to traditional skate shoes at mile 80—and changed to a lower board at the same time—then enjoyed the final 20 miles. Because two things changed together, that account cannot isolate the shoes as the cause.[2]

Editorial conclusion. Compare fit and comfort over long rides before choosing a shoe category.[2]

Wear depends on what you do with the shoes

Community accounts. The Speedforce wear discussion compares substantial sole wear after roughly 600 miles with other users reporting much longer service. The follow-up identifies foot-braking, model differences and rubber grip as possible differences between experiences. These anecdotes cannot establish a universal lifespan, and old warranty comments are not current warranty terms.[4]

Small discomforts can become the limiting factor

Community accounts. Riders name chafing, blisters, hot feet and foot numbness among the things that disrupted otherwise workable plans. Packing discussions mention spare shoes, dry layers and anti-chafe supplies. These are reports about preparation and comfort, not diagnoses or treatment recommendations.[1] [3] [5] [6]

Clothing has practical consequences

Community accounts. A rider’s mistakes list includes a large puffy jacket that felt slow in wind, and several shoe changes that helped them continue. That account puts layers, weather and foot comfort alongside board choice.[7]

Editorial conclusion. Use it to build your own tested options rather than copying a full kit list uncritically.[7]

A familiar size may not mean a familiar fit

Community accounts. The rider who preferred Merrell barefoot shoes described needing days or weeks to adapt to the thin soles. Their usual European size 42 became 43.5 in those shoes. That is one person’s fit report, not a conversion chart or an adaptation timetable for someone else. It adds a practical detail to the conflicting minimalist-shoe experiences above: compare the actual fit, not just the size printed on the box.[2]

Make it practical

Editorial prompts based on the discussion above.

  • Use long rides to check shoe fit, sole wear and grip interaction.
  • Record where friction or discomfort appears.
  • Decide which dry layers or spare shoes are worth having available.
