Choose what a good day means to you

Community accounts. One participant used three targets: a minimum, a realistic goal and a stretch goal. Their first set was 100/120/150 miles; the next was 100/150/200 miles or staying for the full 24 hours. These are personal targets, not an entry requirement or a reason to continue when unwell.[2]

Plan time off the board as well as time on it

Community accounts. Several riders describe being overambitious about maintaining their normal riding speed for an entire day. One inline participant describes starting with a 15 mph pact and falling apart after six hours. Another first-timer returned to finish a 100-mile goal after a long overnight stop. These accounts make room for changing the plan, rather than treating a break as automatic failure.[2] [4]

Use preparation rides to answer concrete questions

Community accounts. A first-timer’s preparation thread covers regular and mongo pushing, switch stance, foot-braking on the weaker foot and riding in different conditions. Its replies discuss reducing unnecessary foot shuffling.[1]

Editorial conclusion. Treat these as skills to explore before the event, with a setup and pace you can control; the thread is not a requirement to master every technique before entering.[1]

Write down your practical plan

Community accounts. Use the packing and first-timer discussions to decide how you will get drinks and food, where your equipment will live, what happens if you need rest, and how you will leave after the event. The community accounts are most useful as prompts for your own plan, not a promise that any particular distance will follow.[3] [4] [5]

Take the next question further

Editorial conclusion. Continue with what should my training help me discover, which rules must I confirm for my edition and discipline, what should I pack, and why.

Make it practical

Editorial prompts based on the discussion above.

  • Write a minimum, realistic and stretch goal.
  • List what you have already tested on long rides and what remains uncertain.
  • Decide who handles resupply, or how you will handle it yourself.
  • Plan a place to rest and transport after the finish.
