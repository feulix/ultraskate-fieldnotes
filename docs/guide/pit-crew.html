Give support a job description

Community accounts. In a parent’s first-time support thread, experienced participants describe preparing bottles, mixing supplies and handing over food and drinks. One contributor recommends practising handoffs; another mentions a small sling bag for supplies. These accounts turn ‘being there to help’ into concrete tasks. Current event rules still determine where handoffs can happen.[2]

Separate the rider’s needs from the crew’s

Community accounts. Replies remind the support person to bring their own food, drinks, seating, layers and somewhere to rest. Family-logistics contributors describe how long and tiring the event can be for non-riders too. A comfortable crew base is part of the plan, not an afterthought once the rider is ready.[2] [3]

A solo plan can be organised too

Community accounts. A solo-rider account describes arranging prepared bottles by flavour and keeping plain water accessible. This is an example of reducing the decisions needed during the ride. The exact location, container rules and access arrangements need confirmation with the organiser for the relevant edition.[4]

Treat venue details as questions to confirm

Community accounts. The support thread contains different descriptions of parking near pits, access to electricity, packet pickup and overnight music. Some clarify that the parking lot is not the pit row itself. Rather than blending those statements into one authoritative description, use them as a list of questions for the current organiser.[2]

Separate parking, pit space and shared facilities

Reported experience. Replies to a first-time supporter initially described parking as being in the pit. Another clarified that cars were in a lot starting a few hundred feet from pit row; a third recalled spaces reserved for camping the previous night. One thought arriving earlier could shorten the walk to the garage. Ask about the exact place for the car, the equipment and the handoff separately, rather than assuming the word “pit” describes all three.[2]

Reported experience. Power was reported along the wall between the track and pit, with limited outlets and a need to share with neighbours. That does not establish available electrical load or permission for a cooking appliance. At Dutch Ultra, a participant described tents and party tents holding food, drink and equipment; no pitch allocation or dimensions were given.[1] [2]

Reported experience. A Miami attendee recalled refill water being supplied in recent years and discussed both bottle swaps and hydration packs. Confirm the refill point and how you will reach it before deciding what water to transport. Their estimate of daytime drinking was personal planning advice, not an individual fluid target; see the limits of copied hydration advice.[2]

Rehearse requests as well as the handoff

Reported experience. One experienced attendee valued practice handoffs because the pit straight was smooth, often had a tailwind and followed a corner exit that carried speed. A forced stop there cost momentum. Those are historical observations, not a reason to rush a handoff or assume the same wind, surface or traffic next time.[2]

Reported experience. A reply proposed small walkie-talkies so a rider could request food, drink or equipment before arriving, explicitly recommending testing them before the event. Another rider prepared fuel the night before and handed it over in a small shoulder sling bag. The bag’s return also needs an agreed procedure; their report of tossing it over a wall is not a general handoff instruction.[2]

Editorial conclusion. Rehearse the request, preparation, transfer and return with the equipment you intend to use. Include a way to stop safely when the transfer is missed, and follow the event’s permitted assistance area.

Make it practical

Editorial prompts based on the discussion above.

  • Agree on resupply, communication and rest responsibilities.
  • Label the rider’s supplies and keep the crew’s supplies separate.
  • Confirm pit layout, access, power and handoff procedures.
  • Prepare a fallback if support needs a break or equipment fails.
