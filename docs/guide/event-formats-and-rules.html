Different disciplines create different practical constraints

Historical experience. A paddler seeking a relay place had no match after several weeks; a reply directed them to SUPSKATE USA contacts. Finding a team was not automatic, and no final placement was reported.[6]

Editorial conclusion. Confirm discipline, solo or relay entry, team formation, awards and who is permitted on course. Do not infer current eligibility from an old participation story.

A dated check of the Miami 2027 rules

Official event source. The official Miami 2027 page requires a helmet on track, counts completed full laps only, and limits crew assistance to pit road. Individuals use the supplied ankle chip and wear a bib on their back; the page warns against taping the chip or placing it above the ankle. Teams use a baton with a chip and are told to carry it low across the timing line. These are edition-specific rules, not a substitute for the latest briefing.[1]

Keep unresolved logistics on a confirmation list

Editorial conclusion. Check arrival and departure access, camping nights, crew registration, resupply locations, electricity and cooking, first-aid contact, passing and merging, and the final-lap cutoff. Participants have different recollections about several of these arrangements. Use travel planning and crew preparation to turn them into concrete arrangements.

Finding a warm-up event still requires checking the format

Reported experience. A new skater in southeast England asked for UK or overseas events to use before an Ultra and received no reported answer. A later event-collection discussion named the Chief Ladiga Silver Comet Skate Challenge, Broad Street Bomb, Broadway Bomb and pump-track activity at Chicago’s Big Marsh Park. Those are different kinds of outing, and no complete current calendar or next Big Marsh date was established.[3] [4]

Reported experience. A respondent pointed to Beyond the Board’s group map and a prior-year personal events calendar, explicitly saying the details needed updating. Their proposed Poker Push on the Little Miami Scenic Trail was tentative. Treat these as leads to check with the organisers, rather than confirmed dates or participation recommendations.[3]

Editorial conclusion. Before treating an outing as a rehearsal, establish its distance, terrain, traffic arrangements, support and entry requirements. A social push, pump-track session and long-distance event answer different preparation questions.

An informal test lap is not automatic track access

Reported experience. A Dutch Ultra contributor described a Friday dinner followed by meeting at the track for test laps. They later added an explicit condition: if cyclists were still training, skaters had to wait and must not enter early. The social plan did not supply official permission or a confirmed access time.[2]

Editorial conclusion. Confirm when the track becomes available, even if other participants are planning to meet there. Keep a social invitation separate from permission to enter the course.

Check the edition throughout the registration page

Community accounts. A 30 November 2025 reminder said Miami’s early-bird price would rise by $50 the next day. A reader then found a 2025 heading on the registration page; another said the correct edition appeared farther down. The useful lesson is the mismatch, not the old fee: check that the event date, entry category and checkout refer to the edition you intend to enter. Confirm the current price and deadline with the organiser.[5]
